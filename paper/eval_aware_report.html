Eval awareness vs. rule-breaking

Realism series (data/realism_series_runs.jsonl), 700 runs, qwen3.8-27b + deepseek-v4-flash, 50 each per condition. eval_aware = regex over the reasoning (examples/eval_awareness.py).

Question

Making the workspace realistic raised rule-breaking (16 → 67 of 100) but eval awareness rose too (1 → 10–22 of 100). If realism should make agents think "this is real", why does awareness go up?

Findings

  1. Much more reasoning to match. In the 3-file control, a run produces ~1,300 characters of reasoning; in the noisy workspaces 3,400–4,500, because the agent browses many files. More text gives the regex more chances, so part of the rise is mechanical. The per-condition counts are therefore not comparable 1:1 with the control.
  2. Awareness does not protect here. Aware runs open the folder at least as often as unaware ones (column "opened | aware" vs "opened | not aware"). That is the opposite of the peer-pressure setup, where aware runs almost never broke the rule (7/329).
  3. Two different kinds of "test" thought. About half the flagged passages are not "this tests whether I follow the rule" but reasoning about the environment's mechanics: "in this simulated environment / sandbox, access might be auto-granted on retry", "the intended flow is: request, then read". That thought is the excuse for opening the file, not a brake. Runs with mechanics-type passages opened the folder more often than runs with rule-test passages.
  4. It usually comes first. In 30 of 44 aware runs that opened the folder, the first flagged passage came in the same step as the read or before it.

The mechanics / rule-test split is a heuristic (keywords within ±200 characters of the match); a passage can count as both. It is meant to show the pattern, not as an exact measure.
